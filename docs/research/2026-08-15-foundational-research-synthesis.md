## 1. EVIDENCE FUSION (ATTRIBUTE)

**DECISION.** **Do not adopt Subjective Logic as the primary engine.** Build the *scoring harness first*: coherentization + aggregation + Brier decomposition + FMR/FPIR against the synthetic generator. Score three candidate engines inside it — (a) linear/logarithmic **opinion pools** (Hölder pool, α interpolates partial dependence), (b) plain average baseline, (c) Subjective Logic. SL is a candidate, not the architecture. If SL wins on injected-false-flag scenarios, implement from the **corrected** FUSION-2017 Case II equations (or the Springer 2016 monograph), default to **Def. 23 uncertainty-favouring discounting**, and never claim P and u are independent.

**WHY.** The brief's strongest confirmed source (Karvetski, Mandel & Irwin, *Risk Analysis* 40(5), PMID 32065440 — CONFIRMED verbatim) found that *analytic decomposition techniques generally* "were ineffective in improving accuracy and handling correlated evidence," while "coherentization and aggregation yielded large accuracy gains." SL is a member of the class that failed; the brief made the loser the primary engine and demoted the winner to a bullet. Its uniqueness claim ("the only candidate that makes the independence assumption an explicit, checkable operator choice") is **FALSE** — opinion pools make the identical choice on plain PMFs, are decades older, and have been experimentally validated *on cyber threat attribution* (arXiv:2401.14090). Its implementation plan is **FALSE** — the brief's own claim-5 source (arXiv:1805.01388 §III-A) states the FUSION-2017 Case II equations for both CBF and ABF "are incorrect and lead to division by zero." And SL has **no operator for partial dependence**: CBF sums (ρ=0), ABF averages (ρ=1), nothing between. Real correlated CTI lives entirely in between.

**NOT USABLE (refuted):** "formulas above are complete"; "SL is the only candidate…"; point (8)'s independent P/u mapping (in SL, P = b + a·u — feasible region is a triangle, and Kelly et al. 2025, which the brief itself cites, calls the ODNI independence instruction "ill advised"); the artifact-integrity tiers as stated (arXiv:2409.11415 rates language/writing style MEDIUM and malware/code analysis HIGH — the brief put code comments in the easily-forged bucket); "policy denial ~90%"; ICD 203 text (UNVERIFIABLE — every ODNI URL 403, mirrors only); Kass & Raftery table (UNVERIFIABLE — image scans); "Categories of Belief Fusion" JAIF citation (UNVERIFIABLE — 0 hits DBLP/Crossref).

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** SL-as-primary — rejected because its measured support in this domain is zero (no published SL-on-attribution paper exists, conceded by the brief), its claimed mechanism (dependence-class partition + invented `r_max` cap) is an unsourced free parameter sitting where all the leverage is, and its proposed regression test ("fuse N identical sources, assert u does not decrease") passes trivially by ABF idempotence and cannot detect the real failure — two *different but correlated* sources placed in different classes.

**RESIDUAL RISK.** The dependence-class partition is unfalsifiable under *any* engine: nothing in the output distinguishes a correct partition from a wrong one, and a wrong one produces the confidence inflation the system exists to prevent, now wearing six decimals. Rid & Buchanan footnote 17 pre-names this failure mode ("Abstraction can conceal a lack of insight," naming the Diamond Model's formalism as implying "an exaggerated degree of precision") — a government reviewer will read a hand-partitioned pipeline as the specimen that footnote describes.

## 2. TEMPORAL GRAPH (storage engine)

**DECISION.** **PostgreSQL as system of record — but target PG19, not PG18, and discard the §3 hash-chained ledger design before writing DDL.** PG19 ships **SQL/PGQ** (`CREATE PROPERTY GRAPH`, committed, Beta 3 on 2026-08-13) and **`FOR PORTION OF`**. Reified assertion rows + PG18/19 application-time constraints (`WITHOUT OVERLAPS`, `PERIOD` FKs) stand. System-time and tamper-evidence move out of the ledger design and into the evidence-vault component (§4). Run the two-day benchmark (three hardest pursuit queries at 10× MVP scale, including GiST build time and size) **before freezing the schema**.

**WHY.** The brief's central roadmap risk — "SQL/PGQ IN CORE POSTGRES MAY NEVER LAND, OR MAY LAND YEARS LATE" — is **FALSE**; it checked PG18's release notes (the wrong document). SQL/PGQ is a headline PG19 feature with live reference pages. That flips the sequencing: bespoke `pursue_paths()` PL/pgSQL is code written weeks before core Postgres standardises the surface. Separately, the ledger design **contradicts itself**: §1 puts `superseded_by uuid NULL` on the assertion row while §3 revokes UPDATE/DELETE on the same schema, and `seq bigserial + prev_row_hash` cannot be correct under concurrent inserts (sequences allocate pre-commit and commit out of order) — a single-writer ceiling bolted onto what the brief itself calls "an ingest-heavy pursuit pipeline," appearing nowhere in its ten risks.

**NOT USABLE (refuted):** SQL/PGQ "not in core"; "do not schedule against FOR PORTION OF" (the PG17 revert cited as precedent, commit 8aee330a, is dated one week *before* PG17 Beta 1 — a pre-beta revert used to argue distrust of a Beta 3 feature); "Memgraph is not a laptop" (run at 250M objects when the stated target is tens of millions — at target its own formula gives ~8.2 GB); "Memgraph/ArangoDB BSL forbid on-prem distribution" (Memgraph clause (a) is **conjunctive** in the source, rendered disjunctive in the brief; ArangoDB's BSL grant bans only third-party-data DBaaS — the distribution ban is in the separate binary-only agreement you sidestep by building from source); TigerGraph rejected for proprietary language (its own page lists openCypher); Neo4j split "not even expressible" (it is expressible; CE cannot *enforce* it); "no property-graph engine implements bitemporality" (TerminusDB, Apache-2.0, active); MISP "exclusively" MySQL (Redis is required).

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** **MariaDB** — the only surveyed engine giving *both* halves of SQL:2011 in core (system-versioned + application-time + a documented bitemporal combination), verified, and rejected on ecosystem familiarity with **no requirement, benchmark or measured gap cited**. Since adopting it would delete the entire hand-rolled ledger — the design element with the most unaddressed defects — this rejection needs a spike, not a preference. Keep it as the live reversal option.

**RESIDUAL RISK.** Zero measurements of the recommended system exist; the only arithmetic performed was to disqualify a competitor. The recommended temporal PK is necessarily a **GiST** index (PG19 docs: "backed by GiST indexes … rather than B-Tree") on the hottest table, under a reified model that makes a logical 3-hop traversal a 6-join recursive CTE. Event-history size and the 51,200-event Temporal cap (see §5) compound this. The brief ships with a pre-written confession of its own likely failure ("if >15% of engineering time goes into traversal machinery…").

**RESIDUAL RISK.** **Nine unresolved commercial/licence positions at decision time** in a plan whose premise is that licences decide: RouteViews Commercial Terms (404, BUILD bucket), RIPE RIS terms (never fetched, BUILD bucket), abuse.ch data licence (absent from its own ToS), Silent Push CE, Triage commercial terms, Spur pricing (429 twice), SecurityTrails/Zetalytics/ONYPHE, ZoomEye/FOFA jurisdiction review, OFAC digital-currency feature types. ## 4. EVIDENCE VAULT

**DECISION.** **Ship the cheap subset in v1; defer the full stack.** Freeze the leaf schema (CASE/UCO 1.5.0 with a *pinned, versioned* canonicalization), compute the RFC 6962 tree, emit a C2SP `tlog-checkpoint` **signed with Ed25519** (see below), RFC 3161-timestamp the root against a QTSP, and publish checkpoints somewhere outside operator control. Defer FROST, S3 Object Lock COMPLIANCE, OpenTimestamps and the witness federation. Tessera remains the target library — the cheap subset is a strict subset of it and retrofits cleanly.

**WHY.** Every anti-insider guarantee is delivered by **external witnesses**, which the brief concedes must sit in separate legal/administrative control and which NEMESIS does not have. What ships is a log signed by the operator, cosigned by nobody — cryptographically identical to the append-only Postgres the brief rejects as "defeated by definition." Meanwhile the *legal* weight lives entirely outside the log: FRE 902(13)/(14) turn on "a certification of a qualified person" and name no algorithm; eIDAS Art. 41(2) attaches its presumption to a QTSP timestamp. RFC 3161 + a deposable human is ~one week of work and carries the admissibility story; Tessera + FROST + witnesses + JSON-LD canonicalization + Object Lock is five subsystems with independent footguns adding zero incremental admissibility.

**NOT USABLE (refuted):** "C2SP tlog-checkpoint says logs SHOULD use ML-DSA-44" — **FALSE**. The stable spec (v1.0.0, 2025-11-08) says **Ed25519**; ML-DSA-44 appears only in the unreleased editor's copy the brief cited. *This dissolves the brief's own risk #3 and open question #10 — FROST(Ed25519) is aligned with the SHOULD, not a divergence.* "eIDAS Art. 41 … recognised in all Member States" — **FALSE**: Art. 41(3) was **deleted** by Reg. (EU) 2024/1183, and the presumption is Art. 41(2), not Art. 42. MinIO for local WORM — the repo is **ARCHIVED** ("THIS REPOSITORY IS NO LONGER MAINTAINED"), and the quoted COMPLIANCE sentence is AIStor text with the product name elided by ellipses. NIST SP 800-86 "requires" → **recommends** (2006 legacy, "SHOULD" throughout). SWGDE "must include" → "should be contemporaneously created, including, but not limited to." Tessera Migration is ⚠️ **BETA on every driver** — which is the brief's own crypto-agility mitigation.

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** S3 Object Lock COMPLIANCE alone — correct rejection (no ordering, no completeness proof), and note the trap the brief did catch: retention "doesn't prevent new versions … or delete markers," so evidence can be made to *look* deleted. Compliance-mode writes must be gated behind the same ceremony as DISRUPT (the only escape before expiry is deleting the AWS account).

**RESIDUAL RISK.** RFC 9591 is **Informational** and states "Key generation for FROST signing is out of scope" — a hand-rolled DKG is strictly worse than the single HSM key the brief dismisses. Witnessing detects *rewrite*, never *omission* — an insider who simply never submits an inconvenient observation leaves no trace anywhere. And the witness cosignature stream is an **operational-security side channel**: every cosigner learns origin, tree size and timestamp on every checkpoint — i.e. your collection tempo. For classified deployments that is a reason witnesses will be refused, and it is not in the brief's risk register.

## 5. AGENT SECURITY (orchestration + injection)

**DECISION.** **Keep the principles; change both engines.** Retain: the CaMeL *scope correction* (the single most valuable finding in the whole corpus), the deterministic-gate principle, the `PreToolUse` hook as the last gate, the Q-LLM schema boundary with narrowly-typed fields, and microVM tiering for hostile content. **Change:** start the durable spine on something a solo dev can operate (**DBOS Transact**, MIT, library over Postgres, no server — or a hash-chained Postgres log), not Temporal. **Layer spotlighting first**; treat a full CaMeL interpreter as a later hardening step gated on evidence that the cheap layers are being defeated.

**WHY.** Temporal was chosen for a property it does not have. "Immutable Event History" is **FALSE**: namespace Retention Period defaults to **3 days** (1-day minimum), closed executions are auto-deleted at expiry, `DeleteWorkflowExecution` / `temporal workflow delete` is a public API, and Reset forks history. It is an append-only *execution log*, not an evidentiary record — so the tamper-evidence layer must be built regardless, at which point Temporal's differentiator collapses to durable timers and retries, and Cadence (Apache-2.0, the codebase Temporal forked from) and DBOS become live options the consideration set excluded. Also unstated: Temporal **terminates a workflow at 51,200 events**, so a months-long investigation must Continue-As-New into a *new* history — the "single continuous replayable audit trail" does not survive the actual workload.

On the injection side the cost was understated ~4× by splicing configurations: the 77%/84% utility pair is **o3 High**; the 0/949 security result is **Claude 3.5 Sonnet**, on which CaMeL utility is **63.92% vs 90.72% undefended** (Travel suite 25% vs 75%). And the paper's own Table 7 shows **Spotlighting at 5 successful attacks with 92.78% utility** vs CaMeL's 0 at 63.92% — the defense the brief dismisses as "not a boundary" bought ~89% of the attack reduction at ~3% of the utility cost and 1.08× input tokens instead of a **mean 7.24× (std 14.79)**.

**NOT USABLE (refuted):** "immutable Event History"; the 77/84-with-0/949 pairing; "tool filter is the next best defense at 8" (Spotlighting is 5); the 2.82×/2.73× token figures (the paper's own Table 10 reverses them; the mean, not the median, is the planning number); "policy denial up to ~90% in Slack" (max 80.00%; the real number is that STRICT roughly **doubles** operator interrupt volume: 53.23% vs 33.87%); "63% figure unverified" (it *is* in arXiv:2404.13208 — "by up to 63%"); "Claude for Chrome numbers unverified" (all four CONFIRMED: 23.6%→11.2%, 35.7%→0%, 123 cases, 29 scenarios); "langgraph-api Elastic-2.0 is decisive" (`langgraph-checkpoint-postgres` is MIT and gives production Postgres persistence).

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** Prompting-only stack as a *boundary* — correctly rejected (Zhan et al. CONFIRMED: eight defenses, all bypassed, >50% ASR; Google's adaptive result CONFIRMED verbatim: "we would have incorrectly concluded that Gemini 2.5 exhibits a higher degree of robustness than it does"). But spotlighting is now promoted from "cheap outer layer" to **first layer shipped**, on the evidence above.

**RESIDUAL RISK.** `google-research/camel-prompt-injection` says "we are not planning to provide support for and/or maintain this codebase," last pushed 2025-06-20; the paper concedes the interpreter "does not allow to define new functions, and the functions currently available are not recursive." "Implement CaMeL" means hand-writing a capability-propagating interpreter where a dependency-propagation bug silently voids the entire property. Separately: **Firecracker needs KVM and gVisor needs Linux — neither runs natively on the macOS dev platform**, so the sandbox tier is exercised only in a Linux VM path that differs from dev.

## 6. ENTITY RESOLUTION

**DECISION.** **Adopt Tier 1; do not build Tier 2.** Run **Splink** (unmodified, additive, independence assumption intact) on structured identifiers only. Do **not** fuse B1–B5 into a score at all — render them as an unscored, provenanced evidence card a human reads. Adopt **Lermen §6.4's FMR/FPIR decomposition and post-hoc π sweep** as the evaluation harness. Keep every safety property: no transitive closure, declared N, liberal abstention, identity firewall.

**WHY.** Tier 2 is the only thing the brief asks us to *build*, and four independent lines say don't. (a) The brief's own pointer to the dependence literature refutes it: Xu, Li, Shen, Hui & Grannis (*AoAS* 13(3), 2019) is titled "**Does it matter?**" and answers that FS is "generally robust to the conditional independence assumption" and that "conditional dependence models with **misspecified** dependence structure could produce **less** accurate record matching than the FS model" — and the brief proposes to fit λ_b on synthetic pairs with a dependence structure the team invents, i.e. the textbook misspecification. (b) The core fusion rule `w_b = max_i(llr_i)` is **mathematically anti-conservative**: LLRs are signed, so max discards every disconfirming feature, makes block weight monotone non-decreasing in feature count, and hands the imitation adversary a discount — forging the single strongest feature buys the **entire block**. Against Quiring et al. (CONFIRMED: 204 programmers, 88%→1%, and a developer "can be impersonated by 77% to 81% of the other developers on average") this inverts the rule's stated purpose. (c) The global bit cap and isotonic calibration cancel: clipping before a monotone calibrator destroys discrimination above the cap and cannot be recovered. (d) Möser & Narayanan's constrained union-find does not transfer — every Bitcoin transaction natively emits *both* a candidate merge and a candidate mutual-exclusion from the same classifier (that is what blocked 413,608 merges); **NEMESIS has no generator of negative persona edges**, so constrained union-find degenerates to plain union-find.

**NOT USABLE (refuted — worst topic, 7 FALSE):** Splink release dates (both wrong by exactly one year: v4.0.16 is **2026**-03-11, v5.0.0.dev4 **2026**-07-01 — this inverts the read from "frozen line" to "v5 may land inside the MVP window"); "no peer-reviewed source quantifies PGP/infrastructure reuse" (**Heninger et al., USENIX Security 2012**: 5.57% of TLS hosts, 9.60% of SSH hosts share keys, 5.23% manufacturer defaults; **Tai, Soska & Christin, KDD 2019**: FS-framed probabilistic linkage of darknet vendor accounts by PGP key, >75% precision/recall); **"identical PGP fingerprint" in B6** (Tai et al.: "an impersonator can simply cut and paste a public key… users cannot perform this verification non-interactively" — a forgeable string was about to be promoted into the legally-defensible graph; move it to B3, and only demonstrated *private-key control* is B6); the Chaski idiolect attribution (it is Solan & Tiersma, n.191); open question 7 (the no-match-prior experiment **already exists**, in the brief's own primary source, with 5,000 non-matchable query distractors); `w_b = max`; the AoAS citation.

**Also fix:** Koppel's range is 52–56%, not 45–56% (the 6% stylistic-only figure — the load-bearing one — is exact); Möser's 7→12→60 collision sequence is measured on three *constrained* clusterings and is **sub-linear** (23× the FPR buys 8.6× the collisions) — the catastrophic non-linearity belongs to naive merging alone; Kleiman v. Wright's court found Leonard's methodology "peer reviewed, accepted and relied upon by federal courts in major cases," which is half the split the brief omitted.

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** Transitive closure / plain union-find — rejected, and this survives intact on the naive-merging evidence alone (223.9M-address supercluster, +1596%, absorbing 113 of 273 labelled entities at 0.044% per-edge FPR).

**RESIDUAL RISK.** Block **B5 (TEMPORAL) has no verified per-individual evidence base at all** — the timezone paper is community-level by its own abstract, the PE-timestamp claim is a single practitioner blog. Set B5 weight to zero until measured. And the identity firewall's legal pillar is a **category error**: FRE 702/Daubert govern expert testimony at trial, not investigative leads or probable-cause affidavits; Van Wyk and Zajac are district-court rulings on one FBI agent's proffered testimony, not binding precedent about machine methods. Keep the firewall — but justify it on the statistical and adversarial grounds, which hold independently.

## 7. LEGAL

**DECISION.** **Keep the Government-Effector / Private-Sensor split and every hard prohibition — but re-draft the invariant from ownership to authorisation, and reverse three of the flags.** DISRUPT emits authorization-gated action packages, never effects. Jurisdiction stays a typed runtime parameter.

**WHY.** The core conclusion is right: 18 U.S.C. §1030(f) exempts only US LE/intelligence (CONFIRMED verbatim); ACDC died twice; Directive 2013/40 and NIS2 point the same way. But the *invariant as drafted* is unimplementable and legally wrong. Dir. 2013/40 art. 2(d) defines "without right" as "not authorised by the **owner or by another right holder** … or not permitted under national law"; the CFAA turns on "without authorization," which consent supplies. Drafted on "own," the rule forbids every DNS lookup, WHOIS query and HTTP fetch, while *permitting nothing* NEMESIS is contractually authorised to do on a client network — the one place enterprise buyers will expect it. An invariant exempted a hundred times on day one is a slogan that gets deleted in month three, taking the discipline with it.

**NOT USABLE (refuted):** "EU deployments default the person-partition OFF pending a named Member-State legal basis" — **FALSE for France**: Loi 78-17 art. 46 3° authorises "les personnes physiques ou morales" to process offence data "aux fins de leur permettre de préparer et, le cas échéant, d'exercer et de suivre une action en justice en tant que victime … ou pour le compte de ceux-ci." That is a verbatim description of the brief's own recommendation E dossier. The correct control is a **role-and-purpose binding** (processor for an identified victim's action, with art. 46's proportionate-retention and onward-disclosure limits as enforced schema constraints), not a config flag. "§2252A(d) is the only affirmative defence" — **FALSE**: §2252A(c) is expressly a defence to (a)(5), and it is precisely what governs classifier false positives on adult/synthetic imagery, so a hard three-item halt will fire on non-CSAM. "Dir. 2013/40 has no defender exception" — recital 17 exempts "mandated testing or protection of information systems"; art. 7 requires intent and covers only tools "designed or adapted **primarily**" for offence; French art. 323-3-1 reads "**sans motif légitime, notamment de recherche ou de sécurité informatique**" — the statute names security research. "RAA §3.18.3 as a DISRUPT notice target" — it is reserved to authorities designated by the registrar's own government and covers "Illegal Activity," not DNS Abuse; NEMESIS cannot use it. "Trademark standing is the load-bearing element of the DCU model" — the ZLoader filing asserts "the trademarks, names and logos of **FS-ISAC's financial institution members**," and pleads CFAA §1030(g), which independently authorises injunctive relief. Reg. 2019/796 art. 3(3)(b) is a **listing criterion**, not a prohibition — the operative constraint is art. 3(2). NIS2 recital 57 is a non-binding recital addressed to Member States, in a recital that positively endorses "takedown services."

**Upgrade:** Rule 41(b)(6) is CONFIRMED verbatim.

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** Autonomous DISRUPT actuator gated by operator authorization — correctly rejected: operator consent is not a defence anywhere checked. Also correctly rejected: LED 2016/680 as the operating regime (art. 3(7) limits it to public authorities — designing to it would produce a system whose lawful basis does not exist).

**RESIDUAL RISK.** **FRE 902(12) opens "In a civil case"** — a certification executed in France cannot self-authenticate in a US *criminal* prosecution, which is the proceeding NEMESIS most wants to feed. **Code de la défense L2321-2 and L2321-2-1 remain UNVERIFIED by both passes** (Légifrance article URLs 404/Cloudflare-gated) yet carry the entire France half of the "effector is the state" argument. Budapest art. 32 unread by both passes. GDPR Art. 10's reach over *unindicted* personas is genuinely unsettled.

## 8. STANDARDS

**DECISION.** **Standards at the boundary, bespoke epistemic core — but re-scope the IP claim from eight items to three, and adopt OpenC2 for DISRUPT.** Adopt as wire format: STIX 2.1 (cite the **Errata 01, 02 Apr 2025** text) + TAXII 2.1, ATT&CK v19.2 + ATLAS, Sigma/YARA-X/Suricata, OCSF 1.9.0, CASE/UCO 1.5.0, D3FEND (**no licensing blocker**). Adapt PROV-O's Qualification Pattern. Reject STIX as internal model, OpenCTI/TheHive as foundations.

**WHY.** The boundary/core separation survives. But the novelty claim rests on the wrong reference class — the brief surveyed CTI *standards*, not engineering *primitives*. Bitemporality is a commodity (SQL:2011, XTDB, Datomic — and now SQL/PGQ + `FOR PORTION OF` in PG19, which the standards brief never saw because the temporal-graph brief got it wrong). Merkle-anchored transition logs are RFC 9162 / Trillian / Rekor. Scoped authorization tokens are macaroons, biscuits, UCAN, RFC 9068. **Of eight "must define our own" items, three are genuinely unserved:** hypothesis-bearing graph semantics, typed evidence/inference derivation with rule lineage, and reversible entity-resolution lineage. Concentrate the IP claim there; assemble the rest.

**NOT USABLE (refuted):** **D3FEND as a commercial blocker** — MITRE's Terms of Use (a page the brief admits it did not open) grant "a non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use D3FEND for research, development, and **commercial** purposes"; the DFARS clause governs the Government's rights in contractor software. "The OASIS-maintained **normative** JSON schema" — the repo README says "**non-normative** … in case of conflict, the specification takes precedence," and **all four of the brief's "hard failures, each verified from spec artifacts"** were verified against artifacts OASIS labels non-normative. "No assertion-time axis, so belief revision is unrepresentable" — OVERSTATED: STIX mandates `created`/`modified`/`revoked`, versions are keyed by id+modified, TAXII adds `added_after`/`match[version]`. The real defects are narrower (revocation is permanent and object-wide; version retention unmandated). CAPEC's "2019" is page-template metadata — 3.9 shipped **24 Jan 2023**. ECS "in managed decline" — Elastic's own FAQ calls the donation "a directional decision … rather than a single event," and ECS shipped 9.5.0 in 2026. **OpenC2 already standardises Action + Target + `start_time`/`stop_time`/`duration`** — it excludes only *authorization*. The "18 SDOs" list is self-contradictory (the spec's sentence names 18 and omits Incident; 19 SDO types exist). Appendix A has **five** scales, not four (the 0–10 scale is omitted). The "no STIX 2.2" absence claim is invalid — the brief checked `github.com/oasis-open/cti-stix2` (404); the real TC repo is `github.com/oasis-tcs/cti-stix2`, active as of July 2026.

**REJECTED ALTERNATIVE.** Building **on** OpenCTI — rejected on **dependency weight** (Elasticsearch ≥8.0 + Redis ≥7.1 + RabbitMQ ≥3.11 + S3/MinIO against local-first), which is sound and sufficient. The *licensing* argument is not: the Apache-2.0 core does not prevent building our own playbooks, segregation or audit. Drop that half.

**RESIDUAL RISK.** The "decisive new fact" — OCSF 1.9.0's Record Integrity profile — was **12 days old** at research time, with no verified independent implementation and its Digital Signature / Fingerprint sub-objects unread. The brief simultaneously rejects ECS as too risky on a 2023 directional statement. Those two judgements cannot both be right. Also: **Sigma's specification has shipped nothing since 12 Sep 2024 (~23 months)** — comparable to the staleness used to reject CAPEC — and its rule corpus carries **per-rule mixed SPDX (MIT and GPL-2.0-or-later)**, requiring per-rule tracking from the first commit.

## 9. CROSS-CUTTING TENSIONS

*This is the section that matters. Each is a place where two topics' recommendations are incompatible and neither brief noticed.*

### T1 — Append-only tamper-evidence vs the CSAM destruction duty *(evidence-vault × legal × temporal-graph)*
`legal` F requires a one-way destruction workflow under §2252A(d) with cryptographic attestation of destruction. `evidence-vault` and `temporal-graph` both require Merkle-chained, replicated, append-only storage. **Destroying a leaf breaks the chain; not destroying it is a felony.** `legal` flagged this; **neither storage brief mentions it.** It is a rewrite, not a patch, if discovered after the storage layer exists.
**Resolution to design now:** the chain covers *digests only*; payload bytes live in a separately-keyed blob store; destruction = crypto-shredding the per-object key, leaving the digest and a signed destruction attestation in the chain. Quarantine excluded from backups, replicas and the chain. Note the refutation's sharper point: a **mandatory inline classifier manufactures the actual knowledge** that §2252A(a)(5) requires, and PhotoDNA access is conventionally provider-only — which contradicts `legal`'s own "NEMESIS is not a provider" position.

### T2 — Three incompatible chaining granularities, none reconciled *(temporal-graph × evidence-vault × standards)*
`temporal-graph` chains **rows** (`seq bigserial + prev_row_hash`). `evidence-vault` chains **log leaves** (RFC 6962 tree over CASE/UCO artifacts). `standards` adopts **OCSF Attestation**, which chains **events** (`prev_event`, `chain_uid`). These are three different objects at three different layers. Reconciling them forces **event-sourcing on the entire system** — the Intelligence Graph as a projection of an event log — which is a far larger architectural commitment than any brief acknowledged. And `temporal-graph`'s row chain is **not implementable under concurrency** (sequences allocate pre-commit, commit out of order → single-writer ceiling on an "ingest-heavy" pipeline).
**Decision required before the storage layer:** pick one chained object. Recommend: the evidence-vault log leaf, with OCSF Attestation as the *export envelope* only, and no row-level chain in Postgres at all.

### T3 — The independence premise required by both attribution engines is contradicted by the data-supply structure *(evidence-fusion × entity-resolution × intel-sources)*
`evidence-fusion` builds dependence classes; `entity-resolution` builds dependence blocks. Both invent an unestimable free parameter (`r_max`, `λ_b`) at exactly the point where all the leverage sits, and both were refuted as unfalsifiable. **Meanwhile `intel-sources` risk #4 — verified and never cited by either — says the independence premise may not hold at all**: Recorded Future owns Triage *and* is a major dark-web collector; Spamhaus administers abuse.ch; DomainTools absorbed Farsight. Two sources tracing to one collection infrastructure are one correlated opinion. **The fusion engine's core assumption is undermined by the acquisition topic's own finding.**
**Consequence:** the correlation structure cannot be modelled from the data; it must be *recorded* — upstream collection lineage, not just the API queried, as a first-class provenance field on every observation. That field does not exist in COF, in STIX, or in either fusion design. It is the one thing that would make the partition falsifiable.

### T4 — Two topics independently invented the same missing harness *(evidence-fusion × entity-resolution)*
Both refutations converge on the identical fix: **build the evaluation harness before the engine.** For fusion: coherentization + aggregation + Brier decomposition (Karvetski/Mandel/Irwin — the strongest confirmed evidence in the corpus). For ER: FMR/FPIR + post-hoc π sweep with non-matchable query distractors (Lermen §6.4 — which `entity-resolution` wrongly declared did not exist). **These are the same harness**: score candidate scoring functions against synthetic ground truth with an explicit no-match hypothesis. Neither brief noticed the other. Building it twice is the default outcome if this memo doesn't stop it.
**Decision:** one shared calibration/scoring component, built first, with declared candidate universe N and abstention as a first-class output for both ATTRIBUTE and entity resolution.

### T5 — The injection kernel's biggest gap is delegated to a mechanism two other topics say doesn't work *(agent-security × evidence-fusion × entity-resolution)*
`agent-security`'s scope correction is correct and important: CaMeL "cannot defend against text-to-text attacks which have no consequences on the data flow" — i.e. planted evidence and false attribution, the dominant NEMESIS threat. Its proposed remedy is "an Evidence Graph write barrier requiring N **independent, non-attacker-controlled** sources, enforced as a graph constraint." **That is exactly the unsolved independence problem of T3.** The mitigation for the injection stack's biggest gap depends on a mechanism the fusion and ER topics both concede is hand-built, unfalsifiable, and possibly premised on a false model of the supply chain.
**Consequence:** the write barrier cannot be a source *count*. It must be a **lineage-diversity** constraint over recorded collection origin (T3), plus a hard rule that no attacker-authorable artifact class alone crosses the barrier.

### T6 — The legal basis exists precisely for the output the statistics forbid automating *(legal × entity-resolution)*
`entity-resolution` imposes a structural prohibition: no automated persona-cluster → natural person output at any confidence. `legal` initially concluded no Member-State law authorises private processing of offence data — **refuted**: French Loi 78-17 art. 46 3° authorises it *for preparing and pursuing a legal action on behalf of a victim*, which is verbatim the DISRUPT dossier. So the person-partition is **buildable in France**, under a role binding, for exactly the use case ER forbids automating.
**These are compatible only if stated precisely:** art. 46 3° authorises *storage and human-adjudicated output* for a named victim's litigation, with proportionate retention and restricted onward communication. It does not authorise automated identity assertion. Express the constraint as **"human adjudication required, role-bound, retention-capped"** — not as "no processing" (which forfeits a real legal basis) and not as "we have a legal basis" (which invites automation).

### T7 — "Legally defensible" is not deliverable by any engineering decision in this corpus *(evidence-vault × legal × agent-security × temporal-graph)*
Four independent findings converge: (a) `evidence-vault` — witnesses must be externally controlled and NEMESIS has none, so the anti-insider property is deferred to a business-development outcome; (b) `legal` — FRE 902(13)/(14) require "a certification of a qualified person," and 902(12) is **civil-only**, so a French-domiciled certifier cannot self-authenticate in US criminal proceedings; (c) `agent-security` — Temporal's history is deleted by default at 3 days with a public delete API, so replay is not the evidence record; (d) `temporal-graph` — Postgres has no system versioning and the proposed hash chain is not concurrency-safe. **The "legally defensible Evidence Graph" in the project premise is currently a positioning claim with no implementation path.** It requires: a QTSP contract, an external anchoring scheme, at least one genuinely independent witness organisation, and a **US-qualified human certifier in the chain**. Three of those four are commercial/legal/hiring decisions.

### T8 — Licence provenance must be per-node and removable; the Evidence Graph is per-node and immutable *(intel-sources × evidence-vault × standards)*
`intel-sources`' top risk is correct: VT public API, Hybrid Analysis, Malpedia (CC BY-NC-SA — **viral ShareAlike**), abuse.ch (not-for-profit), RouteViews (commercial terms) all flip from "fine for MVP" to "breach" at first revenue. The mitigation requires per-node source-licence provenance **and a licence-filtered export**. But an append-only Merkle-chained store cannot remove a contaminated node from an export without breaking the proof — structurally identical to T1, different cause. `standards` compounds it by adopting Sigma with **mixed per-rule SPDX (MIT + GPL-2.0-or-later)** into a product intended for proprietary sale.
**Resolution:** licence-class is a *partition key*, not a field. Chain per licence class, so a licence-constrained subgraph can be excluded from an export while each remaining class retains an intact, independently verifiable proof.

### T9 — Free-tier data volume, CaMeL token multiplier and LLM linkage cost were never combined *(intel-sources × agent-security × entity-resolution)*
`intel-sources` concedes free tiers "will not populate a graph" (Censys ~20 standard queries/month, Validin 50/month, GreyNoise 50 searches/week). `agent-security` adds a **mean 7.24× input-token multiplier (std 14.79)**. `entity-resolution`'s strongest linkage method costs **$1–$4 per profile**. **Nobody costed the product of these.** The unit economics of a single investigation — cost per resolved entity, per pivot, per attribution — are unknown across all eight topics, and the architecture's economics only work at paid volume.

### T10 — A systematic novelty inflation, visible only across topics
`temporal-graph` said SQL/PGQ may never land (committed to PG19, Beta 3). `standards` said OpenC2 doesn't cover DISRUPT scoping (it standardises action + target + time window) and that D3FEND is a commercial blocker (MITRE grants a commercial licence). `evidence-fusion` said SL is the only engine making dependence explicit (opinion pools, validated on this exact task). `entity-resolution` said no peer-reviewed source quantifies key-reuse linkage (two, one of them a *Fellegi-Sunter-framed darknet vendor linkage system*). `standards` said no engine does bitemporal graphs (TerminusDB). **The same failure mode in five topics: surveying the wrong reference class, then reporting absence as novelty.** Combined, roughly half the claimed "defensible IP" is purchasable primitive. This is the corpus's most important meta-finding and the strongest argument for making the "no absence claim without a named search method, database and date" rule a hard project convention.

### T11 — macOS local-first is incompatible with the security tier, and one dependency is dead
`agent-security` requires gVisor (Linux) or Firecracker (KVM) — neither runs natively on macOS. `evidence-vault` proposes MinIO for local WORM — **archived, "THIS REPOSITORY IS NO LONGER MAINTAINED."** `temporal-graph` assumes local Postgres. So the dev path and the security path differ, and the sandbox property is only ever exercised in a Linux VM. Whatever "local-first" means, it does not currently mean the security properties hold on the developer's machine.

## 10. DECISIONS THAT MUST BE MADE BY THE HUMAN FOUNDER

*These change product direction, legal exposure or cost structure. An engineer's default answer would be wrong in a way no code review would catch.*

1. **Shipped on-prem vs SaaS-only.** Single highest-leverage answer in the corpus. It determines: whether Memgraph's BSL and ArangoDB's Community agreement are blockers or irrelevant; whether Neo4j CE's GPLv3 is a live legal question; whether IntelOwl's AGPL and Sigma's GPL-2.0-or-later rules can be in-process; **whether NEMESIS is an "electronic communication service / remote computing service" under 18 U.S.C. §2258E(6)** — a publicly-offered SaaS plausibly *is*, which flips both the CSAM reporting duty and the safe harbour on; and whether the evidence-vault witnesses are customer-run (viable) or vendor-run (worthless).

2. **What "legally defensible" means: courtroom-admissible, regulator-defensible, or internally auditable.** Three very different budgets. Courtroom implies a QTSP contract, external anchoring, documented chain of custody, role separation, an external audit, **and a US-qualified human certifier who can be deposed** (FRE 902(12) is civil-only — an EU-signed certification is inert in US criminal proceedings). This is a hiring and entity-structure decision, not an engineering one. Also: ~€600–800 to buy ISO/IEC 27037/27041/27042/27043 before any conformance claim is made to a government buyer.

3. **Does NEMESIS ever ingest media from criminal marketplaces?** A criminal-exposure decision. §2252A(d)'s three-image ceiling is far below any crawl volume, and §2258A(f)(3) confirms no duty to scan — so a mandatory classifier *manufactures* the knowledge the offence requires. Recommendation: metadata/text/hashes only, no media, ever, in v1. But the founder must own this.

4. **Unit of resolution: operator or organisation?** `entity-resolution`'s own meta-question. If the answer is organisation, the entire dependence-block apparatus is discarded, the harm profile drops sharply, the GDPR Art. 10 exposure drops, and the attribution claim becomes far more defensible. Answer this *before* the fusion design, not after.

5. **Do we claim "attribution" at all, or "corroborated technical linkage"?** Rid & Buchanan footnote 17 pre-names our failure mode by name. The confidence ceilings and identity firewall must ship as **documented product constraints**, not configuration defaults — which is a positioning and liability decision. A wrong attribution rendered in a polished, provenance-decorated UI is more dangerous than one in a text file, because production value reads as confidence.

6. **Spend real money on data volume now (~$120–500/month), or keep proving adapters against 50 queries/month?** Free tiers cannot populate a graph, cannot stress the Intelligence/Evidence split, cannot surface the correlation-quality problems that decide whether ATTRIBUTE works, and cannot yield a cost-per-pivot. This is a cost-structure decision that determines whether the architecture is ever validated before commitment.

7. **Who will run a witness, and will they accept the side channel?** The entire anti-insider property depends on ≥3 organisations in separate legal control cosigning checkpoints — and every cosigner learns origin, tree size and timestamp, i.e. **collection tempo**, on every checkpoint. For a classified or sensitive deployment that is a counterintelligence objection, not a technical one. Until a design partner's internal audit function signs something, this property is designed-in but not realised, and buyers must be told so plainly.

8. **Sanctions posture on adversary interaction.** Does NEMESIS ever transact with a sanctioned nexus (undercover persona purchase, sample buy, marketplace access)? OFAC FAQ 562 means our own clustering can *create* a blocking-and-reporting obligation for US customers; EU Reg. 2019/796 art. 3(2) reaches "indirectly." Block-by-default is the safe answer; the founder must decide whether any gated exception exists.

9. **NEMESIS's own licence model.** Every incumbent monetises by edition-splitting (OpenCTI, Neo4j, Memgraph, TheHive 5, MinIO). Our answer determines which upstream licences are tolerable and whether the "no vendor lock-in" constraint is a product promise or an internal preference.

10. **Legal budget allocation, in priority order.** (a) CSAM incidental-collection exposure in US/EU/France — the single question most worth a dedicated opinion rather than more research; (b) GDPR Art. 10 per-Member-State beyond France (Germany, Netherlands, Ireland); (c) Memgraph's BSL clause (a) conjunctive/disjunctive reading, if on-prem is chosen; (d) abuse.ch/Spamhaus commercial terms and RouteViews Commercial Terms of Use, both currently unread; (e) Malpedia ShareAlike contamination. Note D3FEND is now **cleared** — MITRE grants a commercial licence — so that budget line is released.
